# Craft review: “As discussed”

Chapter 11, the FFmpeg/Libav fork — eleven years told from objects. 1,762 words of body
against a 2,200–3,200 band. This is a craft review, not a fact check. Hashes, dates, commands,
and quoted output are locked. I am not touching them.

The bones are the right bones, and they are almost in the right order. A revert with two
spelling mistakes; a how-to that forbids merges; 1,151 `qatar` merges whose modal hour is 02;
a licence string at 0 against 5,042; a heading that outlives its name; a path that exists in
neither parent. The through-line is already a sentence the book has earned: git did not store
the war, it stored the chores the war caused. Three sentences in this draft are keepers for
the series, and two more will be if the captions around them die. I will name them.

The problem is not five objects. Five photographs of one fork is the chapter. The problem is
that the doorway issues the schism as jacket copy, the 2 a.m. histogram is diagnosed in the
same paragraph that prints it, and the narrator stands next to Niedermayer for 1,762 words
while certifying that both sides were principled. Neutrality that has to be announced is a
side. 1,762 is under the floor. That is permission to sit in two Tuesdays the captions skip.
It is not permission to explain a living man to us, and it is not a mandate to add a sixth
exhibit.

That is the whole job.

## Neutrality

**The objects are even. The captions are not.**

Every named person in this chapter is alive. Most of them still commit to this repository.
The prompt asked for a sentence-level audit, no 'coup', both parties principled. 'Coup' is
not in the draft. That is the easy one. The rest of the martial register is still a coat:
*war*, *schism*, *written declaration*, *armistice*, *shooting stopped*, *the wall*,
*epitaph*, *amputated*. After chapter 07, *epitaph* is a wreath. After chapter 04, a man
alone at 2 a.m. is not an innocent clock. You do not owe a callback. You do owe not to
bronze lateness as virtue, and you do owe not to bury one living side under the other
living side's nights.

The draft knows the risk. It handles it by announcing the answer:

> The disagreement was real and principled on both sides, and git records its opening
> moves as what they were: infrastructure chores.

The second half of that sentence is the chapter. The first half is a stamp on the passport.
If both sides are principled, the reader will feel it in two Tuesdays. If you have to say
it, one of the Tuesdays is missing. Cut the stamp. Show Jason Garrett-Glaser at 11:05
citing a thread, deleting seven lines of an org chart he believes the project has already
left. Show the how-to the next room over: merges hide history, so merges are forbidden.
Those are beliefs. They do not need you to call them principled. They need you not to
describe them, two pages later, as an attempted removal.

Because that is what the emotional center currently does:

> For eleven hundred days, the leader whose removal had been attempted sat up at night
> and folded the forkers' work — their codecs, their cleanups, their renames of his
> project — into his own tree, conflict by conflict, so that FFmpeg would contain
> everything Libav had and Libav would have nothing FFmpeg lacked. He would not join
> them and he would not let them diverge. He absorbed them.

"Whose removal had been attempted" is the FFmpeg-side reading of January 18, installed as
fact. The object is seven lines cut from `MAINTAINERS`. The Libav reading is: there is no
such office. Those are not the same sentence. "His project" is a possessive the other
side spent 5,388 replacements contesting. "Forkers" is what you call people from the tree
that survived. "Absorbed them" is a power verb with a winner in it. "So that FFmpeg would
contain everything Libav had and Libav would have nothing FFmpeg lacked" is intent, and
git does not store intent. It stores 1,151 merges. The histogram was about to do the
whole job. This paragraph talks over it, and the talk is a side.

The rest of the sided captions, in the order they arrive:

**"one prodigiously productive successor holding 'final design decisions.'"** Productivity
is not the argument the other side was making. Putting it in the winter-of-2011
establishing shot makes the subsequent governance fight sound like ingratitude. The
1,151 and, if you ever show it, the shortlog will say how much he worked. You do not
need the adjective in the doorway of the disagreement.

**"a developer removes the project leader's office from the org chart."** Accurate as
theatre; slanted as reconstruction. He removed a heading. "Office" personalises the
furniture. The next day the man named under the heading puts the furniture back. Both
acts can be chores. "Office" makes only one of them a home invasion.

**"from a remote he had named not `libav` but `qatar`."** The subject line is
`Merge remote branch 'qatar/master'`. That is enough. The contrast with the name they
chose is a gotcha: he would not type their word. Maybe the remote was a hostname.
Maybe it was spite. The receipts do not say, and this review is not going to guess.
Pointing at the refusal is a sneer you have not earned. Print the subject. Let the
reader notice.

**"You can ask git who won."** Then 0, then 5,042, then "A fork's outcome, reduced to a
grep." The grep is a keeper. "Who won" is a scoreboard, and it is the surviving clone
asking a surviving clone. Four hundred words later the same narrator will say "Not
*he lost*, not *we won*." Those two sentences cannot share a chapter unless the first
one is a trap the second one springs. Right now they are two consecutive endings.
Cut "who won." Keep the numbers. Keep "reduced to a grep." Let Khirnov be the first
person in the chapter who is allowed to refuse a victory.

**"He just takes his name off the door and leaves the door."** That is a beautiful
sentence, and it is a reading. The object is: heading stays, name goes, seven years of
a rule with no one under it. One reading is resignation. The other is that the office
remained as a claim. Khirnov's 2022 commit is the second reading, arriving eleven
years late. If you have already dignified 2015 as a man leaving a door, Khirnov is
tidying a generous gesture. If you leave 2015 blank — a heading, a rule, a missing
name — then "The position does not exist anymore" is a decision, and both sides can
still live in it.

**"look at who signs the commit" / "a man from the other side of the wall" / "written
by a returnee."** The pickaxe already printed `Anton Khirnov`. The wink is the narrator
nudging the irony before the four words are allowed to work. "The wall" is Berlin.
"Returnee" makes FFmpeg the home people come back to. From this clone that is what
happened. From the other tree it is not a return, it is a surviving remote. Name him
when the commit names him. Do not tour-guide the side he used to be on in front of
the sentence that refuses sides.

**"Their directory idea, our file name."** *Our*. That is the narrator joining a
team. It is the one word in the draft I would cut on sight. The merge coined
`fftools/ffmpeg.c`. That is already the photograph. You do not need a possessive.

**At home: "the histogram of its hours tells you what the policy cost somebody."**
Somebody, singular, and then 1,151, 2 a.m., one man. The takeaway of the chapter as
bound is Niedermayer's nights. The other side's years of parallel work are not in
the homework. If the record is what you want believed, believe more of it than one
man's clock.

What is already even, and should be protected:

> same code, same history, different name, different governance — and for the next
> several years the two trees developed side by side, each convinced it was the real
> one.

That sentence does the stamp's job without the stamp. Keep it. It is how you do
neutrality in this book: two beliefs, one clone, no scoreboard.

> Not *he lost*, not *we won*. The position does not exist anymore.

Keep that. It is the chapter's actual ethics, and it is Khirnov's, which is why it
works. Do not also write it in the January doorway as "principled on both sides."
Do not also undo it with "who won." One naming. You currently have three.

The 2015 blank and the 2022 four words are the neutrality the prompt asked for,
sitting in the tree. The captions keep translating them into a story about one
man's endurance. Stop translating.

## qatar is the center. The narrator will not get out of the way.

**The histogram lands. Then a paragraph explains what we were supposed to feel.**

The prompt asked this plainly, so I will be exact.

This is the object:

```
    103 02
     94 14
     90 01
```

103 at two in the morning. 94 at two in the afternoon. 90 at one in the morning.
The modal hour is 02. The next one is 14. That second number is on the page and
the prose never looks at it. "Sat up at night" becomes the whole story, which is
romance, and after chapter 05 this book does not get to bronze the small hours.
A man who did this at 02 *and* at 14 is more devoted, not less. He is also less
convenient as a nocturne. Print both peaks. Do not pick the cinematic one.

Then stop.

The count is 1,151. The author uniq is one name. The first merge is 2011-04-02 at
01:51, seventy-five days after "merge commits are forbidden." The last is a DNxHD
assembler port with two conflicted Makefiles and no farewell. Those four facts are
the emotional center. They do not need a theory of absorption. They do not need
"the strangest sustained habit I know of in any repository" as a drumroll, though
that is at least a person talking, which the rest of the section mostly isn't.
They need the first night inhabited, and they need the last night left alone.

Right now qatar is a caption of commands. First merge, printed. Count, printed.
Author uniq, printed. Histogram, printed. Then the diagnosis paragraph, quoted
above. Then "And then it just stops," which is the right sentence, and then the
last merge, which is the best writing in the draft:

> The last qatar merge is an assembler port with two conflicted Makefiles. There
> is no farewell in it, no announcement, no line drawn. The remote simply never
> appears again.

Protect that. It should have whitespace on both sides. It does not — it shares a
paragraph with a war that had a written declaration and an armistice and "nothing
left worth sitting up for." "Worth sitting up for" values Libav's decline in
Niedermayer's sleep. Cut the martial clause. Keep "the habit ended the way habits
end." That last phrase is a keeper, and it is already the reading. You do not
need "He absorbed them" a screen earlier to prepare it.

The missing room is the first night, not a better caption of the thousandth.

April 2, 01:51. Seventy-five days earlier his own tree was told that merges are
forbidden and the master tree will reject them. The other tree is now a remote.
He types `Merge remote branch 'qatar/master'`. Reconstruct what he knew: their
codecs were landing over there; the how-to said this object should not exist; he
minted one. Do not tell us he would not join them and would not let them diverge.
He merged. The next night he merged. The uniq will do the thousand. The histogram
will do the hours. The last Makefile pair will do the ending. If you inhabit
01:51 without a strategy, the reader will arrive at 1,151 having felt the
obsession a beat before anyone could name it. That is the assignment. The
diagnosis paragraph is the assignment failed in the room where it mattered most.

"Niedermayer's answer to the fork was not to ignore it and not to fight it" is
the same failure one page earlier, as a topic sentence. Cut it. The forbidden
object is the topic sentence.

## Five sections after the open

**January / qatar / string / office / scar. Almost. Swap two, starve two, inhabit
two. Do not add a sixth.**

The argument is one move, run on one clone: the mailing list is gone; the objects
are not. January is the split as chores. qatar is the three-year practice.
String is one claim, exchanged 5,388 times. Scar is a path coined at the join.
Office is sixteen years of a heading. That is not a listicle. That is one fork
answering, five times, *what did git actually keep?* Keep all five names. Keep
the question.

What blurs the set is treating every name as a room, and putting the human last
word in the slot before a git-footgun.

### The order is right until the string, and wrong after the office.

January before qatar is required. You cannot feel 1,151 forbidden objects until
the how-to has forbidden them. You cannot feel "as discussed" as a nothing-phrase
until seven lines have gone and come back in twenty-four hours. Do not reshuffle
the first two.

String after qatar is the deflation. You have just watched a man merge for three
years, conflict by conflict, codecs and cleanups and nights. Then:

> What were they fighting over, object by object? Among other things: a string.

Among other things. The section that follows is 5,388 replacements and a grep.
It is a real photograph. It is the wrong question to ask *after* the nights,
because it makes the nights look like they were about a word. The licence rename
is March 2011. The first qatar merge is April 2. Chronology already wants the
string first: they rename the shared history, *then* he starts absorbing a tree
that has their name in the headers, *then* the tidal re-import makes sense as
what those 2 a.m. conflicts felt like. Move **The string** to sit between
January and qatar, or fold it into qatar as a single photograph — the 1,335-file
commit plus the 0/5,042 grep — without the "who won" frame. Do not let it answer
"what were they fighting over" after 1,151 merges. They were fighting over a
governance heading, a name, a no-merge constitution, and whether the trees would
diverge. A string is one of those. It is not the translation of the nights.

**The scar belongs. It is in the wrong slot, and it is wearing the wrong coat.**

It is not an appendix of content. A path that exists in neither parent, carrying
FFmpeg's bytes under a coined directory, is the war's character in a tree, and
`--follow` going blind in 2017 is the thing this narrator — an engineer who runs
blame daily — should take home. That is book-native. Chapter 07 taught the second
clock. Chapter 10 taught pickaxe on the guards. This chapter gets to teach that
`--follow` cannot see a rename that never happened in either parent. Cut that
and you have thrown away the one habit that is not "go find a fork."

It is an appendix of *placement*. You have just given Khirnov four words that
dissolve the office, eleven years late. Then:

> One object from the reconciliation years, because it is the war's whole
> character in a single merge.

That is a narrator who knows the epitaph was the landing and has one more slide.
"The war's whole character" is a caption stealing from the four words. "The
scar" as a heading is trauma-language for a git limitation. Two different scars
are being asked to be one: the compromise path, which is 2017 and human, and
`--follow`'s amputation, which is a tool being honest about what it cannot see.
Keep the ls-tree. It is a photograph. Move the section before **The office**,
where 2017 belongs, so the join is a coined path and the last human word is
still 2022. Give `--follow` to At home. Rename the heading off *scar* and onto
the path: `fftools/ffmpeg.c`. An object, not a wound.

If you will not move it, starve it. No "whole character." No "that is how this
war actually ended." The ls-tree plus "a path that exists in *neither parent*"
is the ending that section has earned. Then stop, because the office is waiting
and you have already spent its last line once.

**The office is the landing. Keep it last among the human rooms.**

Four commits on a heading, 2006–2022. A 2015 name-deletion the heading-pickaxe
cannot see. A 2022 four-word body signed by a man who had left. That is the
title's payment, if you let "as discussed" live long enough to meet "The
position does not exist anymore." Discussion in, dissolution out. Do not put a
footgun after that. Do not preview 2022 in the pickaxe and then walk it.

Which is the other pacing failure in this section, and it is the same failure
chapter 10 was told to stop: the log is newest-first.

```

111ccca602 2011-01-19 ... Revert "Initial MAINTAINERS update"
f4f5cab94e 2011-01-18 ... Initial MAINTAINERS update
```

The first row is the ending. The narrator then says "four commits, sixteen

That hole is the archaeology. Show the four rows. Let the reader feel 2011 to

Volume, not order, for January and for string-as-photograph. January is an

## The title does not flip.

**"As discussed" is praised in paragraph one, cited twice, and retired. A title
that never comes back cannot invert.**

The open does this to it:

> One sentence of explanation, two spelling mistakes, and a phrase — *as
> discussed* — doing more work than any sentence in this book.

That is a drumroll, a series-wink, and the title spent. The phrase is a
nothing-phrase. It is how a man in a hurry files a paperwork revert: this is not
a new decision, we already had this conversation. Jason's deletion cites a
thread the same way. Both sides entered the tree as citations of a discussion
git does not contain. That is interesting *if the phrase is allowed to sound
like paperwork*. If you tell me in sentence one that it does more work than any
sentence in the book, I am not reading a revert. I am reading a thesis title.

It never comes back. January restates it in italics. Then 1,151 nights, a
string, an empty office, four words. The four words are the flip, and they are
waiting, and nothing in the draft turns to look at them:

> The position does not exist anymore.

No thread. No "as discussed." A fact, four words, eleven years after a revert
that could not quite say one. That is the inversion. Discussion in; dissolution
out. You get it for free if the open does not already tell me the phrase is
load-bearing, and if the office is allowed to be last.

The two spelling mistakes are a separate problem. "mistakely commited" is in
the object; the object is locked. Pointing at both spellings in the first
sentence of prose is cheap character, and it is adjacent to sneering at a
non-native speaker who still commits here. Haste is the point, later, at 01:51.
Literacy is not. If you keep the mistakes, they are how a revert looks when it
is a chore. They are not the joke of the doorway.
