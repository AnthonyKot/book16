# Craft review: “THIS IS A HACK TO GET V4 OUT THE DOOR”

Chapter 15, postgres — the 1992 third, the XXX inverted, the
grammar that only warns. 1,688 words of body against a 2,200–3,200
band. This is a craft review, not a fact check. Hashes, dates,
commands, and quoted output are locked. I am not touching them.

The bones are the right bones, and they are almost in the right
order. A number that is one third; an `XXX` that says *really need*;
a neighboring file that shouts `THIS IS A HACK TO GET V4 OUT THE
DOOR` and dates itself `7/9/92`; a 2015 commit that could replace
the guess and will not; a 2025 blame that puts `0.3333333` back
and calls the oldness `hoariness`; a parser production whose only
job is to fail; a first object that is HPUX loader flags. The
through-line is already a sentence the book has earned: the
comment that said *fix me* is now the comment that says *do not*,
and the refusal is the engineering. Three sentences in this draft
are keepers for the series, and two more will be if the captions
around them die. I will name them.

The problem is not two refusals. A constant he will not change,
and a feature he will not write, is the chapter: one thought, two
photographs. The problem is that the doorway issues the thirty-three
years of nothing as jacket copy, 2015 is a quote with a moral
attached, 2025 is introduced as a law, and The hand is chapter 14's
fade in a quieter coat. 1,688 is under the floor. That is permission
to sit in the two rooms the prompt already named. It is not
permission to write Tom Lane's biography, and it is not a mandate
to catch the `XXX` out.

That is the whole job.

## Does it earn its place next to node and openssl?

**Yes, as archaeology. Not yet as a chapter, if the chapter is 'a
maintainer and an old line of code.' The new thing is a comment
whose instruction inverted, and a syntax that only warns. The draft
keeps spending both as a careful man's résumé.**

The prompt asked this first, so I will not bury it.

Chapter 13 is a deletion that won: a shipped API, two words on a
Saturday, a path that returns zero, folklore that says *never
existed*. Chapter 14 is a deletion that could not leave until a
version was accepted: six subjects, eight bloodless words, an
ellipsis. A reader who has lived both will arrive here primed to
watch another maintainer argue with an old line. If this chapter
spends 1,688 words confirming that priming — Tom Lane, twenty-seven
years, sixteen thousand commits, the hack he would not touch — it
is node-plus-openssl with a planner accent, and it should not ship.
If it spends them on the thing neither chapter had, it is the
reason the book gets a restraint chapter that is not a third
maintainer-and-a-line.

That thing is already in the draft.

**The comment inverted.** Node's leftover is an absence. OpenSSL's
leftover is a revert body that names acceptance. Nobody in those
chapters typed, as the reason for keeping a wrong number, *the
hoariness of this behavior*, or restored a fallback so that the
next person would also refuse. The 1992 author wrote `XXX ...
really need a true selectivity function`. The 2025 comment writes
the opposite instruction into the same estimate. That is unique
in the corpus. It is not "Postgres is carefully maintained." It
is: a `fix me` that survived long enough became a `do not`, and
the surviving is the argument. Protect that. It is currently a
caption on a blame hunk (`Read what he has done here`).

**The parser that only warns.** Chapter 13's second photograph is a
deletion that lost (`we do not plan to remove it`). Chapter 14 has
no companion of this kind. A grammar production for `ALTER TYPE
... DROP VALUE` whose body is `FEATURE_NOT_SUPPORTED` plus a
checklist is not a constant he would not change. It is a feature
he refused to ship, documented at the syntax, so the next person
is guaranteed to find the refusal before they start coding. That
is a different object. It is why the register sentence — the
deepest work is knowing what not to change — is the chapter and
not a caption on `0.3333333` alone. After the third, the refusal
is a number left alone. After `gram.y`, the refusal is a patch.
You need both photographs for the sentence to mean what it says.

They stop cohering here:

> You keep seeing the same name because it is, to a startling
> degree, the same person.

That is a résumé. From here the chapter is secretly about scale:
1998, loader flags, 16,863, most of anyone, a through-line of
judgment. That chapter already shipped twice. It is 07 (one name,
nine thousand commits, the last object is a spelling fix) and it
is 14 (one name, a third of the tree, the last object is `make
update`). If The hand is here to prove that Tom is the person who
does this, you are writing the Henson fade again with a database
accent, and you should not.

Primary, then: the third, the `XXX`, the 1992 shout, 2015 declines,
2025 restores and inverts. Companion: the grammar production that
only fails. The rhyme is not a career. The rhyme is a refusal
written down at the place you would change the thing. Do not write
"the same name." Do not write "to a startling degree." Start the
scale on the object (`Change HPUX loader flags to trap null pointer
derefs`, then 16,863) and let the reader feel the first commit is
not a feature, without being told the through-line of an enormous
body of work. The count can sit, as a photograph of volume — not
as the reason the refusals matter. The 1992 hack, and the enum he
declines, are the reason, and they are already in the draft.

What does *not* earn its place is the maintainer-biography register,
applied to a story whose distinctive objects are a comment that
flipped and a syntax that refuses:

> Twenty-seven years and sixteen thousand eight hundred sixty-three
> commits later, more than any other human in the project's history,
> and the through-line of that enormous body of work is not the
> features he added but the judgment about what not to disturb.

That sentence could close a conference talk about Tom Lane. It
names the genre (career), the verdict (judgment), and the method
(what not to disturb), after the third and the enum have already
done that work as photographs. The unique archaeology is not that
a careful person left a hack alone. Everyone who has maintained
anything has heard that. The unique archaeology is *the `XXX` now
says do not, and the feature is a warning in `gram.y`.* That
sentence is already in the draft, in Tom's words, in two comments.
It is currently a caption on a commit count.

Do not name the rhyme. Do not write "unlike node," "unlike
openssl," "Ryan deleted; Henson was not accepted; Tom declined."
Do not write *careful* as a prize — that is a wreath on the
register the book asked you to inhabit, and it is also the
same-y the prompt named. The shout, the twenty-three years, the
hoariness, and the placeholder production will do the
distinguishing if you let them be photographs instead of a
briefing on how restraint beats cleverness.

The "I might have done this" of this chapter is not 16,863, and
it is not a villainous refactor. It is Tom in September 2015,
with expression-index stats in hand, leaving `0.3333333` where
it was because it had survived, and it is whoever initialed
`JMH 7/9/92`, shouting a hack at a V4 deadline because the
planner needed a number before Monday. A person who ships the
wrong third so the version can leave, and a person who later
will not replace it even though they can, are people the reader
of this book has been. Sit there. Do not sit in a career the
count already printed.

One more distinction, because the fade will try to steal it:
chapter 07's scale is a man about to die. Chapter 14's scale is
a name that stops. Tom's 16,863 is still accumulating. The 2025
restore is last year. He is not leaving. The first commit is
loader flags; the recent one is hoariness; the same person is
still here, still refusing. That is not an elegy. Do not write
one. The prompt asked you to end on the scale. End on it as a
count, not as a wreath.

## Selectivity, for a reader who does not have a planner

**Legible. Almost the right volume. Do not add a primer. Add one
image in 2015, not in the open.**

Present explainer, after the first hunk:

> When the planner sees a query condition it cannot estimate — a
> function call in a `WHERE` clause, something it has no statistics
> for — it guesses that one third of the rows will match. Why a
> third? No reason. It is a placeholder, and it says so.

A non-DB reader can hold that: the database is guessing how many
rows match; the guess is a third; the comment says the guess is
wrong. An expert is not bored, because the photograph is the
`XXX` and the assignment, not a lecture on join order. That is
the same volume as chapter 14's Dual EC (the numbers underneath
the keys; this generator was in the standard) before the FIPS
paragraph ran away. Keep this volume. Do not explain `Cost`. Do
not explain `clauseinfo`. Do not add histograms, `ANALYZE`, or
the difference between `func(x)` and an operator. The comment
is the explanation.

What a general reader still cannot feel is *why leaving it is the
engineering*. "Steered the query plan" and "some better, some
catastrophically worse" are correct and abstract. One image,
once, in the 2015 room, not here: the guess is how many rows a
`WHERE function(...)` will return; the rest of the plan — which
index, which join, whether to seq-scan — hangs on that guess;
change the third and you re-plan queries nobody listed. Then
Tom's sentence. An expert already knows this and will not mind,
because the image is in the service of the refusal, not in the
service of teaching them their job. A non-DB reader who only
has "placeholder" will nod at "he left a hack" and miss "he left
a contract." The ~ needs the image, or the ~ is a proverb about
numbers.

Do not put the image in the open. The open is a third, an `XXX`,
a shout. If the first screen teaches the planner, the shout is a
textbook caption. Teach the cost of changing it where someone is
about to change it.

## Title

**It flips. Paragraph one spends the flip. The all-caps is the
object's voice; keep it. Protect it from the doorway, and from a
contents page that will hear shouting if the chapter also shouts.**

`THIS IS A HACK TO GET V4 OUT THE DOOR` is the 1992 comment, all
caps, initialed, dated. After this chapter it is also the opposite
of a hack: the third that was supposed to leave with V4 is still
the fallback, restored, warned, load-bearing. That is a flip in
the same family as `Fix typo` (it was not a typo) and `Remove
promises` (the folklore was a deletion) and `Remove Dual EC DRBG
again...` (the plot is the *again*). It is louder than those. It
does not ironize the comment. It *is* the comment. The irony is
in the thirty-three years, which 2015 and 2025 produce, and in
Tom's quiet against JMH's shout, which the two comments already
are.

The title only gets to do that work if the first screen does not
explain the thirty-three years. Right now a reader sees the shout
as a title, then is shown a third and an `XXX`, then is shown the
shout again, then is told:

> Somebody at Berkeley, shipping version 4 of the academic
> Postgres, needed a number to make the planner work, could not
> afford to compute the right one before the deadline, wrote
> `0.3333333`, and shouted into the comment that this was a hack
> and someone should really do it properly. Then they shipped.
> This chapter is about what happened to that hack over the next
> thirty-three years, because the answer is: nothing, on purpose,
> and the person who kept deciding to do nothing is one of the
> most careful engineers in open source.

That is the ending, the deadline, the nothing, the purpose, and
the prize, before **The refusal** has started. Chapter 13 put "It
is also wrong, and the repository has known" in this slot and was
told to cut it. Chapter 14 put tired-dots-and-permission in this
slot and was told to cut it. This is that sentence, with a third
in it.

Let it sit as the event. Open on the number, or on the shout,
without the thesis paragraph. The reader who arrives with "hack
means unfinished" sees `7/9/92` and feels the skip. Then 2015,
`not to do`. Then 2025, `hoariness`. Then — and only then — the
hack to get V4 out the door is the door. The title has flipped
in the reader's head, which is the only place a title can.

**All-caps on a contents page.** It will be the loudest title in
the book. Neighbors are sentence case, or a quiet subject (`Fix
typo`, `As discussed`, `Remove promises`, `The current
maintainer`). This one is a man shouting in a comment in 1992.
That is a problem if the chapter also shouts ("most careful
engineers," "to a startling degree," "the hardest and least
visible engineering decision there is"). Then the contents page

restrained. Keep the caps. Do not title-case them; that would
lose the photograph. Do not retitle to `hoariness` (that is the
turn). Do not retitle to `0.3333333` (that is the object, and it
would make the number the title of a chapter whose last object

chapter. Keep it. Let it flip later than the first caption.

One more payment available, and the draft almost takes it: *hack*

The title earns that at 2025, if 2025 is allowed to exist before
the title is called thirty-three years of nothing. Right now
